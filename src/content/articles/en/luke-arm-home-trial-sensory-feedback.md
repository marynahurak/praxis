September 2026 brought a notable development in upper limb prosthetics: the first clinical trial of a neurally controlled prosthesis based on the LUKE Arm system began *in a home setting*. Within this study, the system is referred to as the Utah Bionic Arm.

The first participant to take the system home was Alexander "Avi" Davidson, who lost his left arm in a fall from height 17 years ago.

(The name LUKE Arm is a reference to Luke Skywalker's bionic hand.)

## What is being tested, and what is new

The LUKE Arm multi-articulating prosthesis has existed for some time; the novelty lies in the control interface. Researchers at the University of Utah have integrated the *BIOS Controller*, a direct nerve interface, into the system.

- **Thought-controlled movement.** Electrodes are connected directly to Davidson's residual nerves, enabling intuitive, thought-driven control of the hand and bypassing the limitations of surface EMG sensors, such as skin perspiration and socket displacement.
- **Sensory feedback.** In my view, this is the most important aspect of the study. The interface is bidirectional: the prosthesis's sensors transmit signals to the nervous system, recreating somatosensory feedback. According to Davidson, he can feel how firmly he is squeezing, for example, his wife's hand, and he no longer has to keep looking at the prosthesis while grasping.
- **Take-home trial.** The transition from controlled laboratory conditions to everyday life is a critical stage for invasive technologies. Only in the home setting can the long-term reliability of the electrodes and of the recognition algorithms be assessed during daily activities.

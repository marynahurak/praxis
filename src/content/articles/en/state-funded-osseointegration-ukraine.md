On 7 September, the Ukrainian government adopted [Resolution No. 1095](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/npas/deiaki-pytannia-realizatsii-eksperymentalnoho-proektu-shchodo-oplaty-posluh-poviazanykh-s1095070926), launching a two-year experimental project under which the state pays for osseointegration (OI). The results of the first Ukrainian cohort were published [on 3 October in the peer-reviewed journal International Orthopaedics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00264-026-07054-0). In other words, the funding decision was taken almost a month before the national data were published. This sequence is not a problem in itself: the international evidence base has been accumulating since the 1990s. What concerns me is something else.

At one year after surgery, the Ukrainian results are convincing. In osseointegration, however, the first year is only the beginning. In the landmark Swedish cohort, [the cumulative survival of the fixture at 5 years was 92%, while revision-free survival was only 45%](https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOS-D-17-00621). The follow-up protocol of the pilot project ends at month 12, so we risk measuring only the segment of the survival curve where the figures are still high.

## Mechanics, regulatory framework and study design

### Why direct skeletal attachment changes prosthetic biomechanics

*Mechanistic reasoning (level 6 of the Praxis hierarchy).*

A socket transmits load to the bone through soft tissues, which are subjected to pressure and shear stress. With a short transfemoral residual limb, the lever arm available for controlling the prosthesis is short, and soft-tissue mobility produces pistoning — much like a hydraulic seal that leaks with every step.

Direct skeletal attachment removes this intermediate link: load is transmitted through an intramedullary fixture directly to the bone. Osseoperception emerges, and the range of motion at the hip or shoulder is no longer restricted by the socket brim.

The price is a percutaneous stoma — a permanently open door between the external environment and the medullary canal. In addition, there are the bending moments that arise at the implant–bone interface during falls and the remodelling of bone around the fixture, including stress shielding. At the transhumeral level, axial loads are considerably lower than during walking with a transfemoral implant, so I would expect fewer mechanical — and possibly fewer infectious — complications at this level.

### Conditions of the state pilot project

- **Eligibility:** [patients with short or deformed transfemoral or transhumeral residual limbs who are unable to use a socket because of persistent pain, scarring, dermatitis or ulceration](https://moz.gov.ua/uk/paciyenti-zi-skladnimi-amputaciyami-zmozhut-otrimati-osteointegrativne-protezuvannya-koshtom-derzhavi-rishennya-uryadu).
- **Funding:** [the National Health Service of Ukraine (NHSU) pays for surgery and inpatient rehabilitation under the packages of the Programme of Medical Guarantees and provides an additional UAH 1.2 million for the direct skeletal attachment system. The Social Protection Fund for Persons with Disabilities provides the external prosthesis, components, and the selection, fabrication, fitting and maintenance services](https://moz.gov.ua/uk/paciyenti-zi-skladnimi-amputaciyami-zmozhut-otrimati-osteointegrativne-protezuvannya-koshtom-derzhavi-rishennya-uryadu).
- **Patient pathway** ([as set out in the procedure approved by Resolution No. 1095](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/npas/deiaki-pytannia-realizatsii-eksperymentalnoho-proektu-shchodo-oplaty-posluh-poviazanykh-s1095070926)):
  1. Consultation reports from two prosthetic providers on the feasibility of non-invasive prosthetic fitting.
  2. A decision by the hospital's committee.
  3. Approval of each patient's candidacy by the Ministry of Health.
  4. The patient's written consent. Before obtaining it, the hospital must inform the patient of the risks, alternative methods and the right to refuse.
- **Follow-up:** [check-ups at 6 weeks and at 3, 6 and 12 months after discharge. At 3, 6 and 12 months, functional capacity (a task for the hospital's MDT) and quality of life are assessed and mental health screening is carried out; densitometry is performed at 12 months](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/npas/deiaki-pytannia-realizatsii-eksperymentalnoho-proektu-shchodo-oplaty-posluh-poviazanykh-s1095070926).
- **Participating hospitals:** [according to the Ministry of Health, six institutions have been included](https://medicine.rayon.in.ua/news/1111686-riziki-osteointegraciyi-u-protez-hub-zaklikaiut-moz-pereglianuti-spisok-likaren):
  - Kyiv City Clinical Hospital No. 12;
  - Danylo Halytsky Lviv National Medical University;
  - First Territorial Medical Association of Lviv;
  - Professor M. I. Sytenko Institute of Spine and Joint Pathology;
  - University Hospital of Kharkiv National Medical University;
  - Cherkasy Regional Hospital.

Neither the [procedure](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/npas/deiaki-pytannia-realizatsii-eksperymentalnoho-proektu-shchodo-oplaty-posluh-poviazanykh-s1095070926) nor the Ministry's public explanation sets requirements for the training of prosthetists-orthotists or specifies assessment instruments. They contain only general wording such as 'functional capacity' and 'quality of life', and for the remaining conditions the procedure refers to Ministry of Health Order No. 873 of 26 May 2025. The pilot project covers [25 patients](https://www.kmu.gov.ua/npas/deiaki-pytannia-realizatsii-eksperymentalnoho-proektu-shchodo-oplaty-posluh-poviazanykh-s1095070926) in 2026; at an estimated system cost of UAH 1.2 million, this amounts to UAH 30 million.

### Study design

This is a [retrospective cohort study of 49 veterans of the Russian–Ukrainian war who received the OPRA (Osseointegrated Prostheses for the Rehabilitation of Amputees) system](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00264-026-07054-0). The primary endpoint was implant survival at one year after the second-stage surgery (S2). [According to the manufacturer's press release](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/integrum-new-clinical-study-shows-high-implant-survival-with-the-opra-implant-system-in-war-injured-veterans-in-ukraine-20261014), the median time from amputation to surgery was seven months, considerably shorter than is typical in civilian practice.

## Key findings and critical appraisal

**Findings** ([Bondarenko et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00264-026-07054-0)):

- First-stage surgery (S1): 49 patients, 51 implants — 21 transfemoral, 27 transhumeral and the remainder transtibial (by calculation, 3).
- Three patients declined S2; one transhumeral implant was removed after S1.
- At one year after S2, implant survival was 97.9%: one of 47 implants was removed because of septic loosening. Infectious complications occurred in 18% of patients, including grade 4 infection in 2%.
- After the first year, with a median follow-up of 18.7 months, two further implants were removed: one because of a periprosthetic fracture and the other because of septic loosening.
- [According to the manufacturer's press release](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/integrum-new-clinical-study-shows-high-implant-survival-with-the-opra-implant-system-in-war-injured-veterans-in-ukraine-20261014), overall survival at the time of publication was 93.2%, with no mechanical complications recorded.

**Methodological considerations**

1. **Choice of denominator.** The figure of 97.9% is calculated correctly, but from S2. If counted from the first stage, four of the 51 implants inserted had been removed by the end of follow-up.
2. **Cohort composition.** Most implants were transhumeral, and the evidence for this level is inconsistent:
   - [a prospective FDA study of 12 patients with transhumeral amputation recorded no superficial soft-tissue infections](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/228211);
   - [a Swedish retrospective series (18 patients with transhumeral amputation) recorded 15 superficial infections in 5 patients and skin reactions in 8 patients](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11999-014-3695-6).

   The overall rate of 18%, without a breakdown by amputation level, therefore cannot validly be compared with transfemoral benchmarks.
3. **A retrospective design may underestimate mild infections.** Superficial stoma inflammation treated in outpatient settings is often not captured in medical records. By comparison, [the prospective Swedish transfemoral cohort recorded 70 episodes of superficial infection in 34 patients over 5 years](https://doi.org/10.5435/JAAOS-D-17-00621).
4. **Infection classification.** [In the OGAAP classification used in a recent meta-analysis, stage IV denotes septic loosening of the intramedullary fixture, that is, loss of the implant](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12306-026-00946-9). The 'grade 4 in 2%' probably corresponds to the same removed implant, but the classification used by the Ukrainian authors should be verified against the full text.
5. **Duration of follow-up.** [Long-term data show an implant survival of 72% at 15 years](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31888375/). Complications of osseointegration accumulate over years, not only in the first year.
6. **Early intervention after combat injury** *(mechanistic reasoning)*. A median of seven months from amputation may mean a contaminated wound bed, a risk of heterotopic ossification and a residual limb that has not yet matured. Whether the timing of surgery was a risk factor for infection should be established by analysis of the full text.
7. **Presentation of results.** [The manufacturer's press release](https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/integrum-new-clinical-study-shows-high-implant-survival-with-the-opra-implant-system-in-war-injured-veterans-in-ukraine-20261014) presents these results as evidence of the strengths of both Ukrainian medicine and the product itself. Praxis appraises the peer-reviewed article, not the press release.
8. **Functional outcomes.** The available abstract reports neither patient-reported outcome measures (PROMs) nor functional tests. We know that the implants function, but not how much patients' lives have improved.

## Implications for the MDT

**For prosthetists-orthotists.** A report stating that socket-based fitting is not feasible should not be a formality. I propose a minimum documentation package:

- the number of sockets fabricated, and the types of liners and suspension systems;
- documented wear time;
- photographic documentation of skin condition;
- the Socket Comfort Score (SCS);
- functional tests.

Ideally, both reports should also be issued by providers unaffiliated with the hospital performing the surgery.

**For physical therapists:**

- progressive axial loading according to protocol;
- training in safe falling and in the use of the safety device;
- daily stoma care;
- teaching the patient to recognise early signs of infection.

Densitometry should be performed not only at month 12 but also before surgery; otherwise, the one-year result has no baseline for comparison.

**For the health system:**

- standardised assessment instruments: Q-TFA for the transfemoral level, and DASH and PROMIS Upper Extremity for the transhumeral level;
- a national osseointegration registry;
- follow-up of at least 5 years;
- a clearly defined source of funding for revision procedures.

I support the substance of the [demands that Protez Hub submitted to the Ministry of Health](https://medicine.rayon.in.ua/news/1111686-riziki-osteointegraciyi-u-protez-hub-zaklikaiut-moz-pereglianuti-spisok-likaren):

1. to publish the grounds on which the hospitals were selected;
2. to review the list of hospitals that lack adequate training or equipment;
3. to introduce mandatory training for prosthetists and rehabilitation teams before surgery begins;
4. to guarantee that patients are fully informed.

## Questions for the community

1. Should a report stating that socket-based fitting is not feasible be based on a single minimum protocol? And who should approve it: a professional association, the Ministry of Health, or an expert consensus aligned with ISPO standards?
2. The pilot project runs for two years, but follow-up ends at month 12. Who will collect data at years 3 and 5, and who will pay for revisions when they become necessary?

## Dilemma for the community

**Dilemma (simulated teaching case).** A 28-year-old man with a short transfemoral residual limb after a blast injury; three sockets in one year; chronic dermatitis. What would be your next step?

- New socket: different liner or suspension, documented 3-month trial wear.
- Referral for OI under Resolution No. 1095: socket options exhausted.
- Soft-tissue surgical revision first, then a new socket.
- Decision only after standardised MDT assessment (SCS, Q-TFA).
